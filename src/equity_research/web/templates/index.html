    📌 How to enter a buy — read once
    • One row per buy. Bought the same stock several times (averaging)? Add each buy — use ＋ Another buy on its line. You see one total (qty, average price, P&L), and each buy keeps its own date for tax.
    • Know the date? Enter the quantity and price as you bought them (your contract note). Splits and bonuses since then are applied for you — e.g. 100 @ ₹500 bought before a 1:5 split shows as 500 @ ₹100.
    • No date? Enter what your broker shows today — quantity and average price. Nothing is adjusted.
    • Don't mix them: today's 500 @ ₹100 with the old date would count the split twice — you'll see a ⚠️ if it looks like that.
    • Your company merged into another? Enter the old company as you bought it — type its name (it's listed as “merged”). The tool reads the merger from the filings (e.g. 3 new shares for every 2) and converts it, keeping your cost and date.
    • Demerger? Your cost is split the way the company's own notice says (e.g. 85% stays, 15% goes to the new company) and the new company's shares are added for you automatically — same buy date, and they follow any later change to your buys or sells.
    • Sold some? Click Sell on the stock's line (date, qty, price — tick buyback if you tendered in one). Your oldest shares go first, as the tax rules say, and the gain lands in Booked below by year.
    • Bonus shares appear on their own: ₹0 cost and their own date for tax (your broker averages them in — the totals match). Rights issue? Nothing is assumed — if you subscribed, one click adds those shares.
    • Bought before Feb-2018? The 31-Jan-2018 price counts as your cost for tax (grandfathering) — automatic.
    • ETFs, SME shares, REITs, InvITs and BSE-only shares can be added too — type the name.
    • Why add the date: short- vs long-term tax (held over 12 months), yearly return, the Nifty 500 since, dividends received and XIRR — and raise 50000 can then estimate the tax.
